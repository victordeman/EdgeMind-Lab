## Steps

<Steps>

<Step title="Install the Arduino IDE">

Follow the official guide linked under References to download and install Arduino IDE 2, then start it.

</Step>

<Step title="Connect the board and select it">

1. Plug the board into your computer with the USB cable.
2. Choose your board with **Tools > Board**. Check your kit for the board model.
3. Choose the board's port with **Tools > Port**.

</Step>

<Step title="Run the built-in Blink example">

Open **File > Examples > 01.Basics > Blink** and click **Upload**. The on-board LED should switch on for one second and off for one second, repeatedly. If it does, your board, port and IDE all work.

</Step>

<Step title="Read and upload your own sketch">

Create a new sketch with **File > New Sketch**, replace its contents with the sketch below (or download it), and click **Upload**. It does the same job as the built-in example, with the timing in a named value.

<CodeFile file="/code/arduino-blink/blink.ino" />

- `setup()` runs once when the board starts. `pinMode(LED_PIN, OUTPUT)` makes the pin an output.
- `loop()` runs again and again. `digitalWrite` sets the pin `HIGH` (LED on) or `LOW` (LED off), and `delay` pauses for a number of milliseconds.
- `LED_BUILTIN` is the number of the pin the on-board LED uses. Most boards connect it to digital pin 13.

</Step>

<Step title="Change the timing">

Change `INTERVAL_MS` to `250`. Before you upload, predict how many blinks per second you will see. Upload and check your prediction, then try `100` and `2000`.

</Step>

<Step title="Optional: blink an external LED">

Unplug the USB cable first. The sketch drives the pin that `LED_BUILTIN` names, so an external LED on that pin blinks along with the on-board one. On an UNO that pin is 13; on any other board, check its documentation.

<Wiring>
  <Connection
    from="Board pin named by LED_BUILTIN"
    to="One end of the 220 ohm resistor"
    notes="Pin 13 on an UNO."
  />
  <Connection
    from="Other end of the resistor"
    to="LED long leg (anode)"
    notes="The long leg is the positive one."
  />
  <Connection
    from="LED short leg (cathode)"
    to="Board GND"
    notes="Any GND pin."
  />
</Wiring>

Plug the USB cable back in. The external LED should blink with the on-board LED.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Expected outcome

<ExpectedOutcome>

The on-board LED is on for one interval and off for one interval, repeatedly. When you change `INTERVAL_MS` and upload again, the blink rate changes as you predicted. With the optional circuit built, the external LED blinks in step with the on-board LED.

</ExpectedOutcome>

## Troubleshooting

<Troubleshooting>
  <Issue
    symptom="The port you expect is not listed under Tools > Port"
    cause="The USB cable may carry power only, the board may not be plugged in, or your computer may not recognise the board yet."
    fix="Try a different USB data cable, replug the board and open the Tools > Port menu again. Check your board's documentation for any driver it needs."
  />
  <Issue
    symptom="The upload fails with an error"
    cause="The wrong board or port is selected."
    fix="Choose your board under Tools > Board and its port under Tools > Port, then upload again."
  />
  <Issue
    symptom="The sketch does not compile"
    cause="A typo, such as a missing semicolon or bracket."
    fix="Read the first error line in the IDE's console; it names the line to check. Compare your code with the downloadable blink.ino."
  />
  <Issue
    symptom="The external LED stays dark"
    cause="The LED is the wrong way round, or a wire is not connected."
    fix="Unplug the USB cable, swap the LED's legs so the long leg faces the resistor, and recheck each connection against the wiring table."
  />
</Troubleshooting>
